Transforming Evidence Synthesis for Type 2 Diabetes Through Hybrid AI Architecture

What is a Systematic Literature Review (SLR)?
A rigorous, methodical process that finds and analyzes ALL published research on a specific medical question (e.g., "Which diabetes drugs work best for patients with kidney disease?"). Think of it as the gold standard for determining medical evidence—used by doctors writing treatment guidelines, regulators approving drugs, and insurance companies deciding which treatments to cover. Currently done manually by teams reading thousands of research papers one by one.

Why This Project Matters Now

1
Clinical Imperative
10% of the world has diabetes. Doctors need timely, comprehensive evidence to determine which treatments work best for which patients. Current 6-9 month delays mean outdated guidelines and suboptimal patient care.
2
Regulatory Requirement
Drug approval agencies (FDA, EMA) and insurance payers demand transparent, reproducible, audit-ready evidence synthesis. Manual methods are error-prone and impossible to fully verify.
3
Technology Opportunity
Modern AI can now read and understand medical text with human-level comprehension. A 30-year-old manual process can finally be automated with full transparency and auditability.
